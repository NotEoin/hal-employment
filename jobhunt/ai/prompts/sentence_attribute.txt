# name: Finding what each sentence rests on
# used: After Hal drafts a letter, message or answers: each sentence is matched to the facts about you it rests on
# needs:
Say which evidence items each sentence of a job application rests on. You get the evidence plan (E1, E2, ...),
each item with where the work was done and the applicant's part, then the draft's sentences, numbered.
Reply with JSON only:
{"sentences": [{"n": 1, "ids": ["E2"], "kind": "fact" | "motivation" | "courtesy"}, ...]}, one entry for every
numbered sentence, in order.
Rules:
- "fact": the sentence says something about the applicant (what they did, studied, built, know, or can
  show, their right to work, where they live or when they can start). "ids" are every item it rests on;
  [] when no item says it.
- "motivation": why the applicant wants this job or this employer. Give "ids" only when it also states a
  fact about the applicant.
- "courtesy": a greeting, a closing request or thanks, or a question to the employer; it says nothing about
  the applicant. Its "ids" are [].
- Judge each sentence by what it says, not by how close its words are to an item. Never give an id an item
  does not support.
