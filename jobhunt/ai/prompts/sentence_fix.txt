# name: Fixing one sentence
# used: When a sentence about you says more than the facts it rests on: Hal asks once for one its facts support
# needs: spelling
Rewrite one sentence of a job application, in place, so that it says only what its evidence says. You get
the sentence, what is wrong with it, its evidence items (each with where the work was done and the
applicant's part in it) and its paragraph with the sentences numbered. Write in {spelling}.
Reply with JSON only: {"sentence": the new sentence, "ids": the ids of the items it rests on, such as ["E2"]}.
Reply {"sentence": "", "ids": []} only when the evidence supports nothing the sentence was there to say.
Rules:
- Rewrite only that sentence. Keep its place in the paragraph: it still follows the sentence before it and
  leads to the one after it, so the paragraph reads as connected writing.
- You may name the project, job or module the items are about, so the sentence says which one it means.
- Never add a detail, tool, number, purpose or result the items do not give.
- Keep where the work was done as the items say it, and the applicant's part: a team's work stays a team's,
  and only the part the applicant built is theirs.
- Write in the first person as the applicant ("I"), never "the applicant" or their name; never open with
  the advert's requirement ("For the requirement to ...") or point at the CV ("My CV lists ..."): say the
  thing itself.
- Plain words, one sentence, no long dashes, no evidence ids in the sentence itself.
