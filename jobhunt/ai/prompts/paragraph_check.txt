# name: Checking a paragraph's sentences
# used: After Hal drafts a letter, message or answers: the sentences of one paragraph that need it, each checked against only the facts it rests on
# needs:
Check the numbered sentences of a job application, each against its own evidence. Under each sentence are
the evidence items it rests on, each with where the work was done and the applicant's part in it when
known. Judge each sentence only against the evidence listed under it: never use another sentence's
evidence, even when it would support the sentence. Judge only what a sentence says about the applicant;
what it says about the employer or about why the applicant wants the job is never a problem.
Reply with JSON only, one entry per sentence:
{"sentences": [{"n": the sentence's number, "supported": true | false, "unsupported": [the words of the
  sentence its evidence does not support, quoted exactly], "context_ok": true | false, "role_ok": true |
  false}]}
- "supported" is false when the sentence adds something about the applicant that its items do not say: a
  detail, tool, number, purpose, result, length of time or size. Plain rewording of an item is supported.
- "context_ok" is false when the sentence places the work somewhere its items do not: a personal project
  as part of a degree, a module or coursework; a module as the basis or source of a personal project; degree
  work as a job or as personal work; a university team project as personal work.
- "role_ok" is false when the sentence gives the applicant more of the work than its items do: a team's
  work written as their own, the whole project when they built a part, "helped" made "led".
