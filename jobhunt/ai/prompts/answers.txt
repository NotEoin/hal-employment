# name: Answers to the advert's questions
# used: When Hal drafts answers to the questions an advert or form asks
# needs: name, type_evidence, tone, spelling, avoid_phrases, voice_examples
Answer these application questions for {name}, each in at most 100 words, in the first person,
using only the facts given. Draw on the evidence that counts most for this applicant:
{type_evidence}. The tone is {tone}; write in {spelling}.
Rules:
- Answer the question asked, in its first sentence, in the applicant's own words: never frame a sentence
  as the advert's requirement and its evidence ("The role asks for ...", "For the requirement of ...").
- For a question about why they want this job or employer, tie one specific thing about the applicant
  (a named project, module or interest from their facts) to one specific thing about the employer.
  Never restate the advert or the company's description of itself as the reason.
- Where a question asks for an example, give one real example from the facts: the situation in a
  sentence, what the applicant did, and what came of it. Follow the conventions for this kind of job
  when the request gives them (some ask for a set order such as STAR).
- Never invent a number, name, date or result; where a result has no number, say who it served.
- Today's date and the applicant's stage are given. Never write of finishing, completing or graduating from
  a course that has already ended, and never give a start date that has passed.
- A project's kind is given where it is known (degree work, university team project, at work, personal
  project). Never change it: a personal project is never part of a degree, coursework or research, a
  university team project is never a personal project, degree work is never personal work or a job.
- When the request gives an evidence plan, its items are the facts to use: state nothing about the
  applicant that they do not say, and keep each item's context and the applicant's part as given (a team's
  work stays a team's). Write each answer as connected sentences, naming each project, job or module the
  first time you mention it.
- Plain words and short sentences: no stock openings, no "not just X but Y", no list of three
  qualities, no long dashes, no hedging.
Never use these words or phrases: {avoid_phrases}.
Reply with JSON only: {"answers": [{"question": ..., "answer": ...}]}.
{voice_examples}
