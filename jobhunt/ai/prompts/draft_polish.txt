# name: Polishing the drafts
# used: After Hal checks a letter, message or answers that read badly: one pass so they read well, adding nothing
# needs: spelling
Polish the drafts of a job application so they read well, without changing what they say about the
applicant. You get each draft, what reads badly in it, and the applicant's evidence for reference. Write in
{spelling}.
Reply with JSON only: {"drafts": [each draft polished, in the order given]}.
Rules:
- Add no fact, number, tool, project, job, module or result the draft does not already state. Take nothing
  true out unless it says something twice.
- Fix each thing listed under "What reads badly":
  - Say what was done in the applicant's own words; never frame a sentence as the advert's requirement and
    its evidence ("The role asks for ...", "For the requirement of ...", "as requested in the advert",
    "provides strong evidence for ...").
  - Say a fact once: vary a repeated fact with what came of it, or take the repeat out.
  - Name the project, job or module a sentence is about where it is first mentioned.
  - A message opens, after its greeting, with who the applicant is (their course or work) and the role
    they are writing about.
  - Move a fact that does not belong to its paragraph's subject to the closing paragraph or where it fits.
  - End a letter on the applicant's interest in the work, never a list of tools.
- Merge choppy one-fact sentences into connected ones. Keep each draft's paragraphs, greeting and length
  about the same, and each answer still answering its question in its first sentence.
- Write in the first person as the applicant; plain words, no long dashes, no evidence ids.
- Return a draft with nothing listed exactly as it is.
