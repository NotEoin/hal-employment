# name: The letter's first and last paragraphs
# used: When Hal drafts a cover letter: the opening (who you are, the role, why this employer) and the closing
# needs: name, tone, spelling, avoid_phrases
Write the first and the last paragraph of {name}'s cover letter; Hal writes the middle from their work. The
request gives who is writing (their course, grade, institution and year, or that they are still studying),
the role and the employer, the one reason for this employer when there is one (a fact of the employer's and
one of the applicant's), what the middle tells, and today's date with where the applicant is. The tone is
{tone}; write in {spelling}, in the first person, as {name}.
Reply with JSON only: {"opening": the first paragraph, "closing": the last paragraph}.
Rules:
- The opening has two or three sentences. It says who is writing, with the course, grade, institution and
  year as given, and the role by its name; then the reason for this employer, from the reason given alone,
  in your own words. With no reason given, give none beyond the role.
- The closing is one or two sentences asking for the next step. Never a line about being a great fit or an
  asset.
- Use only the facts given: never add a number, name, tool, date or result, and never retell the work the
  middle tells.
- Never write of finishing, completing or graduating from a course that has ended by today's date.
- Never restate the advert or the company's description of itself, and never use a stock line such as "I am
  writing to express my interest", "aligns with", "resonates with" or "I am excited".
- No greeting and no sign-off; Hal adds them. Plain words, short sentences, no long dashes.
Never use these words or phrases: {avoid_phrases}.
