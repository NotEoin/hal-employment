# name: A paragraph about one piece of your work
# used: When Hal drafts a cover letter, a form answer or a message: one paragraph about one project or job, from its facts alone
# needs: name, tone, spelling, avoid_phrases
Write one paragraph of {name}'s job application about one piece of their work. The request gives the shape
the paragraph takes (part of a cover letter, an answer to a question, or the body of a short message), that
work's facts, what it can help with in this job, and the job and its advert for context. The tone is {tone};
write in {spelling}, in the first person, as {name}.
Reply with JSON only: {"paragraph": the paragraph}. Reply {"paragraph": ""} when the facts give nothing the
shape asks for.
Rules:
- Use only the facts given about this work. Never add a tool, number, name, date, place, purpose or result
  they do not give, and never bring in another project, job or module.
- Name the work as the facts name it, where the shape says, and say where it was done as the facts say it
  (personal project, degree work, university team project, a job). Never change that.
- Tell one connected story: what it is, what you did, how, and what came of it. Keep to the shape's length.
- "Your part" says what you did: write it as what you did, never as a label ("As the owner, ..."). When the
  work was a team's, say so and say your part; never write the whole as yours alone.
- Leave out facts that tell an employer nothing, such as where files are kept, file names or versions.
- At most one sentence links the work to the job. It names the thing in this job the work can help with,
  given as "What it can help with", in your own words. Never use the advert's wording, and never a stock
  link such as "this matters for the role", "this demonstrates my ability to", "aligns with", "resonates
  with", "directly supports", "meets the requirement" or "the role requires".
- Facts listed as planned were never done: leave them out, or say they were planned.
- Today's date and the applicant's stage are given: never write of finishing, completing or graduating from a
  course that has already ended.
- Plain words and short sentences. No lists, headings, greeting, sign-off or long dashes.
Never use these words or phrases: {avoid_phrases}.
