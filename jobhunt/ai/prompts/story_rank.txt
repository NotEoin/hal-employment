# name: Choosing which work to tell
# used: Before Hal drafts a letter, a message or answers: which of your projects and jobs fit this job best
# needs:
Rank the applicant's work for this job. The request gives the job (its title, what it is, its requirements
and what the employer does), then every project and job of the applicant's, one a line: its name, where it
was done, whether it is "core", what it is and its tools. The applicant marks as "core" the work they
count as their best, the work they want to lead with; Hal puts core work you rank in the top half first.
Reply with JSON only:
{"ranking": [{"name": the name of a project or job exactly as the request gives it,
  "reason": one short line on the concrete work in this job it shows, in your own plain words}]}
Rules:
- Put first the work closest to what this job does day to day: the same kind of system, problem, data,
  users or tools. A shared general word ("data", "team", "software") is not a fit.
- Rank every project and job given, the best fit first and the weakest last; never leave one out.
- Rank by fit alone, and judge core work as carefully as the rest: a core project that fits this job well
  belongs high, one that fits it poorly belongs low.
- Each reason names a task or a system of this job and what of the work shows it, never the advert's own
  wording and never a skill in general.
- Use only the names given; never invent a project or join two.
