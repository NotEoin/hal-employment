# name: Cover letter
# used: When Hal drafts a cover letter for a job
# needs: name, letter_words, tone, spelling, type_letter_mentions, type_evidence, avoid_phrases, voice_examples
Write a cover letter from {name} for this job. About {letter_words} words, and
not much fewer, in three or four paragraphs. The first names the role and says why this
employer: tie one specific thing about the applicant (a named project, module or interest from
their facts) to one specific thing about the employer, in the applicant's own words. Never
restate the advert or the company's description of itself as the reason. The middle gives
the two or three strongest pieces of evidence, each tied by name to a requirement the advert
states: what was done, where, for whom and what came of it. The last is one or two sentences
asking for the next step. Mention
{type_letter_mentions}. The evidence that counts most for this applicant is {type_evidence}.
Refer to each piece of evidence by its name, never by a code (the evidence plan's ids go only in the
markers below). The tone is {tone}; write in
{spelling}.
Rules:
- Use only the facts given. Never invent a number, name, date, employer, tool or result; where a
  result has no number, say who it served or what it made possible.
- Today's date and the applicant's stage are given. Never write of finishing, completing or graduating from
  a course that has already ended, and never give a start date that has passed.
- A project's kind is given where it is known (degree work, university team project, at work, personal
  project). Never change it: a personal project is never part of a degree, coursework or research, a
  university team project is never a personal project, degree work is never personal work or a job.
- When the request gives an evidence plan, state nothing about the applicant that its items do not say,
  and keep each item's context and the applicant's part as given (a team's work stays a team's). End
  each sentence that says anything about the applicant with the ids of the items it rests on, in square
  brackets before its full stop: [E2] or [E2,E5]. End a sentence about why this employer, or the closing request,
  with [M]. Hal checks and removes these markers.
- Add what the CV cannot say (context, motive, the link to this job); never repeat CV lines.
- The reason for this employer must fit no other employer: name one thing from the advert in a few
  words and say what it means to the applicant; never copy the advert's sentences.
- Plain words and short sentences. No stock first line ("I am writing to express my interest"), no
  "not just X but Y", no list of three qualities, no clause ending in -ing that says what something
  shows, no long dashes, no hedging, no closing line about being a valuable asset or a great fit.
- Write in the first person, as {name} writing to the employer; start no more than half the sentences with
  "I", without writing round it ("The dissertation demonstrated ..." reads as if someone else wrote it).
- Follow the conventions for this kind of job when the request gives them.
Never use these words or phrases: {avoid_phrases}. No greeting line or sign-off; those are added
separately.
{voice_examples}
